● Evidence

We show the working.

Anyone can claim an accuracy number. These three charts are the ones that actually matter, drawn from four seasons the model was never trained on.

Is it calibrated?

When the model says 70%, does it win 70% of the time? Points on the diagonal mean yes.

predicted →↑ actual

Every held-out prediction is dropped into a 10-point bucket. For each bucket we compare the average predicted probability against the share that actually won.

A perfectly calibrated model sits on the diagonal. Ours tracks it closely where there's real sample size — the 0.75 bucket predicted 74.7% and won 75.3%. The lowest bucket has only 20 games, so it bounces around.

Model vs the market

Accuracy by season against Vegas and against always picking the home team.

Walk-forward testing: to score 2024, the model trains only on 2015–2023 and predicts 2024 cold. No future information ever leaks backward.

Vegas is scored on the same games by converting the closing spread to a win probability. It wins every season — that gap is expected and we don't hide it.

What the model leans on

Each feature's influence, measured as its coefficient times its spread across real games.

influence on the pick

The model is a logistic regression, so influence is |coefficient × standard deviation| — how much a feature actually swings predictions across real matchups.

Elo dominates because it compresses every past result into one number. The efficiency stats then adjust for recent form.

● Power ratings

Elo, from scratch

Margin-of-victory weighted, regressed each offseason. 1500 is league average. No borrowed rankings.

Every team starts at 1500. After each game the winner takes points from the loser, scaled by how surprising the result was: change = K × mov × (result − expected) with K = 20.

Expected result comes from the rating gap plus 55 points of home advantage, about 2.2 points of spread. Each offseason ratings pull 25% back toward 1500 for roster turnover.

● Predictability

Who tips their hand

On downs 1 to 3, how confidently the model can call run or pass before the snap. Higher means easier to read.

Predictability is the average of |p − 0.5| × 2 across a team's plays. Always-obvious offences score high; ones that keep defences guessing score low.

Fourth downs are excluded — punting on 4th-and-15 is not a tendency, it's just football.
